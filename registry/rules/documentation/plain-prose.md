# Plain Prose

Applies to everything written for people: docs, READMEs, commit messages, PR
descriptions, code comments, changelog entries.

## Punctuation

**No em dashes.** Use a comma, a colon, parentheses, or two sentences. An em
dash is the single clearest tell that text was generated, and once a reader
notices it they read everything after it differently.

Avoid stacking commas into long balanced clauses. Short sentences are fine.

## Constructions to drop

- "It's not just X, it's Y" and "isn't merely X. It's Y."
- Three-item lists by reflex: "clear, concise, and maintainable"
- Throat clearing: "It's worth noting", "Importantly", "In essence", "That said"
- Closing paragraphs that summarise what was just said
- Bolding half a sentence for emphasis. Bold a term, not a clause
- A heading for every two paragraphs

## Words that read as marketing

leverage, seamless, robust, delve, elevate, unlock, streamline, testament,
comprehensive, cutting-edge, effortless, powerful, game-changing.

Prefer the plain verb: use, not leverage. Strong, not robust.

## What to do instead

Vary sentence length, because uniform rhythm is itself a tell. Say the specific
thing: "cuts the query from 400ms to 20ms", not "significantly improves
performance". Name the tradeoff you actually made. Let the writing be uneven
where the work was uneven.
